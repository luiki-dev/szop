# ADR 0052 — Working with Claude within the weekly limit

- **Status:** ✅ Accepted
- **Date:** 2026-10-26
- **Extends:** [ADR 0004](0004-implementation-process.md), decision 8 (a phase on the full path runs as one session per stage, see decision 2)

## Context

The owner works on a Claude Code Pro subscription, and its weekly limit ran out before the week ended several weeks in a row. The goal is one account that lasts the week at the owner's usual pace. The Opus session that coordinates the work is the part that must last: when it runs out, everything stops. Two alternatives were looked at first: the next plan up, Max, at about 6× the price, and a second account coordinated by the first, which Anthropic support confirmed is allowed (decision 1).

The [token usage audit](../audits/2026-10-26-token-usage.md) read every Szop transcript up to 2026-10-26 and weighted the tokens in units of one Opus input token. About 273M units were spent; cache reads made up two thirds of it, and the main session 78%. The median main-session call carried 202k tokens of context, and 66% of main-session cost was spent at 200k of context or more, where a call costs two or more times what it costs below 100k. A phase ran in a median of two main sessions, and the longest sessions peaked at 500–700k. **Cost tracks how long a session runs, not how big the phase is:** every call re-reads the whole context from the cache, so a session's cost grows roughly with the square of its length. A simulation over the same transcripts estimated that restarting at each stage of a phase saves about 20% of main-session cost, and also capping a session at about 150–200k of context saves about 30–35%.

The owner sees coordinating the work well as a skill to learn, not only to apply. The [design](../superpowers/specs/2026-10-26-working-with-claude-design.md) holds the reasoning in full, and the guide `docs/development/working-with-claude.md` explains the habits and their mechanics.

## Decisions

| # | Topic | Options considered | Decision and reasoning |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | How to make the limit last | The Max plan; a second Claude account coordinated by the first; Codex on a Plus plan as the worker; one account used more sparingly | **Make one account last, and park the second account as the candidate [OP-109](https://github.com/luiki-dev/szop/issues/296)** _(the owner's choice)_. Max costs about 6× for more than is needed. A second agent adds coordination, and the coordinator remains the bottleneck. Savings on one account help either way. |
| 2 | Sessions in a phase | One session per phase (today); one session with `/compact` at every stage boundary; a session per stage | **A session per stage of a phase: brainstorm and spec together, then the plan, then execution, then finishing (tracking, PR). Each stage starts a new session that reads the committed spec or plan** _(Claude's proposal, the owner's choice)_. Each stage leaves a committed document the next stage can start from, so a new session loses nothing that matters. A new session starts at about 48k of context, a compacted one at about 57–68k carrying a lossy summary. The brainstorm stays with the spec because the reasoning behind the spec exists only in that conversation.<br>➕ **Extended by [ADR 0059](0059-session-skills.md), decisions 2 to 5:** a stage hands over to the next session with `/handoff`. |
| 3 | Long stages | No cap; auto-compact only; restart or compact at a threshold | **Within a long stage (mostly execution), restart or `/compact` when the context passes about 150–200k** _(Claude's proposal, the owner's choice)_. Above 200k, every call costs two or more times what it costs below 100k, and 66% of main-session cost was spent there. A restart costs about one or two calls at 400k. During execution, the plan and the commits on the branch are the state, so a new session can continue from them. <br>➕ **Extended by [ADR 0061](0061-token-usage-evaluations.md), decision 3:** the threshold is kept; its reasoning, a restart's cost against a longer session's, is in the guide. |
| 4 | Auto-compact | The default window of 1M; a lower threshold such as 200k; 300k | **Auto-compact at 300k for Opus 5.5, as a safety net** _(the owner's choice; already set with `/autocompact`)_. It catches a session that was left running too long. A threshold at 200k would compact sessions that are about to end anyway, mid-task, when a restart at a stage boundary would be cleaner. |
| 5 | Breaks | Leaving the session open; finishing the stage first | **Before a break longer than an hour, finish the stage or end the session** _(Claude's proposal, the owner's choice)_. The prompt cache lives one hour. After that, the next call writes the whole context again at 1.25×. These rebuilds cost 4.9% of the total. |
| 6 | Subagent models | The rule in CLAUDE.md only; the default subagent model only; both | **Implementer subagents run on Sonnet, and the whole-branch review runs on Opus. The rule goes in CLAUDE.md, and the default subagent model is Sonnet (`CLAUDE_CODE_SUBAGENT_MODEL` in the project's `.claude/settings.json`), so a dispatch that names no model uses Sonnet** _(Claude's proposal, the owner's choice)_. The default covers a coordinator that forgets the rule. The rule says when Opus is worth it, so the coordinator names it explicitly for the review. An explicit `model` in a dispatch still wins over the default. The saving is about 4–5% of total. It is small, but it also keeps the Opus share of the limit for the coordinator. |
| 7 | The status line's context | Colors by percentage of the window (today: yellow from 50%, red from 75%); the tokens with colors by absolute thresholds | **The status line shows the context in tokens after its percentage (`[#         ] 13% 131k`), colored by absolute thresholds: green below 150k, yellow 150–200k, red above 200k** _(the owner's choice)_. With a 1M window, 50% is 500k, far past the point where calls become expensive. The thresholds match decision 3. |
| 8 | Recording the limit's usage | Reading the percentages by hand; nothing at all; a log written by the status line | **The status line also appends the limit's usage to a comma-separated values (CSV) log, `~/.claude/usage-log.csv`, at most once a minute** _(Claude's proposal, the owner's choice)_. The 5-hour and weekly percentages are the only true measure of the limit, and Claude Code shows them but does not keep them. The log connects them to a session and a branch, so a phase's share of the week can be read back. |
| 9 | Measuring | Comparing weeks only; no measurement; a report per phase against a baseline | **A script, `scripts/token-usage.py`, reports any phase's usage from the transcripts and the CSV, against the [baseline](../audits/2026-10-26-token-usage.md#baseline) of the token usage audit. The usage is judged after 3–4 phases** _(Claude's proposal, the owner's choice)_. Weeks differ in how much work they hold. A phase is the unit of work, and the leading indicators show whether the habits are followed even before the totals move. |
| 10 | The report in a phase's PR | A hook or CI job; only on request; a step in the checklist before the PR | **Before opening a phase's PR, Claude runs the report and puts a "Token usage" section in the PR description** _(the owner's choice: semi-automatic)_. CI cannot see the transcripts, and they live only on the machine that ran the session. A step in the existing checklist costs nothing to remember, and the numbers stay with the phase they describe. The script can also be run by hand at any time. |
| 11 | How long transcripts are kept | The default of 30 days; 365 days | **365 days** (`cleanupPeriodDays`, already set) _(the owner's choice)_. The baseline and every later report are computed from transcripts, and the default would have started deleting the baseline's first files within days. |
| 12 | Where the habits are explained | A section in `phase-walkthrough.md`; a guide of its own | **A guide, `docs/development/working-with-claude.md`** _(the name is the owner's choice)_. The walkthrough explains what happens in a phase as process. This guide is about coordinating the work well, a skill of its own. |
| 13 | The size of a phase | A smaller limit on tasks per plan; a limit on lines of plan; no change | **The size of a phase stays as [ADR 0014](0014-roadmap.md), decision 6, sets it; this restates it and does not change it** _(Claude's proposal; the owner asked whether phases should shrink)_. The transcripts show cost tracks session length, not task count. Sessions per stage address that directly. The evaluation after 3–4 phases can revisit this. |
| 14 | Targets for the evaluation | No targets; targets for the leading indicators and the limit | **Median context per main-session call below 120k; share of main-session cost above 200k below 20%; per-phase cost at least 25% below the baseline median; the owner not running out of the weekly limit before its reset** _(Claude's call; the owner can change the numbers in this ADR)_. Without a target, "it works" cannot be told from "it changed". The first three are leading indicators. The last one is the success criterion itself. <br>➕ **Extended by [ADR 0061](0061-token-usage-evaluations.md), decisions 1 and 2:** evaluations repeat every few phases, cover the whole period with the sessions outside phases, and are recorded in a data file and a page. |

## Consequences

- A phase on the full path runs as one session per stage, each started with a short prompt that names the committed spec or plan. Within execution, a session is restarted or compacted at about 150–200k of context, and before a break longer than an hour the stage is finished or the session ended. CLAUDE.md's section "Working with Claude" holds the rule, and the guide the reasons.
- The status line, `~/.claude/statusline-command.sh`, shows the context in tokens with the colors of decision 7 and writes the CSV log. Both live outside the repository; the guide shows their relevant parts.
- Every phase PR gets a "Token usage" section with the output of `scripts/token-usage.py --branch <the phase branch>`, a step in CLAUDE.md's "Before opening the PR".
- A subagent dispatched with no `model` runs on Sonnet. A coordinator names Opus explicitly for the whole-branch review and for a task the plan marks as hard; an implementer that fails a task twice on Sonnet is retried on Opus, and the PR's token usage section notes it.
- Risks:
  - The cost units are not the subscription's meter; the CSV of real percentages is the check, and the evaluation compares both.
  - Transcripts live on one machine, so a phase worked partly in a cloud session or on another computer is under-counted; the report says what it found, and the PR notes the gap.
  - The transcript format is undocumented and can change with a Claude Code update; the script fails loudly on a missing field instead of reporting zeros.
  - Sonnet implementers may need more retries, which can eat the saving; the retry rule and the Opus share in the report make this visible.
  - Habits erode in long sessions; the status line's colors are the reminder that does not depend on memory.
  - Setting the default subagent model touches `.claude/settings.json`, which holds the deny rules ([ADR 0012](0012-security-baseline.md), decision 19); only an `env` key is added.
- The habits are evaluated after 3–4 phases against the targets of decision 14, tracked by [OP-110](https://github.com/luiki-dev/szop/issues/298). The findings go into the guide; changed targets get a new ADR or an audit of their own.
