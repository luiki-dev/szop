# ADR 0030 — Domain and base infrastructure details

- **Status:** ✅ Accepted — decision 9 refined by [ADR 0042](0042-releases-details.md) (the lifecycle policy's rule 1 shields `*.*.*`); decisions 6 and 13 refined by [ADR 0044](0044-landing-page-details.md) (the apex's CAA record also allows Let's Encrypt, and `szop` has a record of its own; the page is on GitHub Pages and `szop.dzwoniec.com` does not redirect to it)
- **Date:** 2026-10-16
- **Refines:** [ADR 0008](0008-hosting.md), decisions 6 (the certificate leaves base, see decision 5), 12 (the registry's lifecycle, see decision 9) and 17 (the domain, the app's name and the certificate, see decisions 1, 2 and 5); [ADR 0010](0010-ci-cd.md), decisions 11 and 16 (the app's name; the deploy role manages the session's certificate, see decisions 1 and 5)
- **Completes:** [ADR 0008](0008-hosting.md), decisions 22 (secrets never in state, see decision 10), 24 (SES's identity, DMARC and SPF, see decisions 7 and 8) and 27 (the log group, see decision 11)

## Context

[PH-10](../roadmap.md#ph-10-domain-and-base-infrastructure) builds the permanent part of `infra/base`: the domain's zone, the email identity, the image registry, the Better Auth secret and the log group. [ADR 0008](0008-hosting.md) set the direction: base holds the registry, the zone, the certificate, the email identity, the secrets and the log group (decision 6); images live in ECR with a lifecycle policy (decision 12) and immutable tags (decision 13); a domain registered through Route 53, with a certificate for `demo.<domain>` in base (decision 17); app secrets in SSM Parameter Store, never in Terraform state (decisions 21 and 22); SES with its DKIM records (decision 24); and a log group with 7-day retention (decision 27).

Those left the details to the phase, among them which images the lifecycle keeps ([OP-013](https://github.com/luiki-dev/szop/issues/66)), the rule that keeps secrets out of state ([OP-016](https://github.com/luiki-dev/szop/issues/75)) and SES's DMARC and SPF records ([OP-044](https://github.com/luiki-dev/szop/issues/76)). The brainstorm also found two things the earlier decisions had not considered: the domain may later hold other apps, so Szop's name should be its own rather than "demo", and ACM renews a certificate only while it is in use, which a certificate kept in base between demos is not.

This ADR records the brainstorm's decisions, with what was settled while building them.

## Decisions

| # | Topic | Options considered | Decision and reasoning |
|---|-------|--------------------|------------------------|
| 1 | The domain and the app's name | `demo.<domain>` on a domain bought for Szop ([ADR 0008](0008-hosting.md), decision 17); `szop.<domain>` on a domain that may later hold other apps | **`dzwoniec.com`, with Szop at `szop.dzwoniec.com`.** The domain is Szop's only for now, but another app may get its own subdomain later, so Szop's name is its own name rather than "demo". Refines ADR 0008, decision 17. |
| 2 | Registrar and DNS | Route 53 for both; Cloudflare as registrar, which requires Cloudflare's DNS; any registrar with the domain or `szop.` delegated to Route 53 | **Route 53 for both**, as ADR 0008, decision 17 planned. Everything stays in one account and one Terraform provider, with no extra credentials, and `szop` can be an alias record to the load balancer. Cloudflare sells .com about $4.50 a year cheaper, but its DNS would need the Cloudflare provider and an API token stored on the owner's machine and in CI, against the "no stored keys" rule of [ADR 0010](0010-ci-cd.md). Delegating `szop.` to its own zone would separate future apps best, but adds a manual step and a second zone for a need that does not exist yet. |
| 3 | Which module owns the hosted zone | `infra/base`; `infra/bootstrap`; a new `infra/domain` | **`infra/base`**, as ADR 0008, decision 6 planned, **with `prevent_destroy`**, the pattern of the state bucket. A new zone would get new name servers and take the whole domain offline until the registration is updated. A future app finds the zone by name and adds its own records from its own code. Bootstrap would blur its "state and budget" purpose; `infra/domain` would be a module for one resource. |
| 4 | Adopting the zone the purchase creates | An `import` block; a new zone plus the registration managed by Terraform (`aws_route53domains_registered_domain`) pointing at it; a data source only | **An `import` block** with the zone's ID, which the owner reads after buying the domain. The first plan shows the zone imported, not created, and the name servers never change. Importing existing resources is a common task worth learning. Replacing the zone needs a second provider region (`us-east-1`) and a name-server change; a data source would leave the zone and its protection outside the code. The block stays after the first apply, with a comment: it does nothing once the zone is in state and records where it came from. **The registration's settings** (auto-renew, transfer lock, privacy protection) are set by the owner at purchase and stay outside Terraform. <br>**Settled while building:** the first plan is "1 to import, 12 to add, 1 to change": the imported zone is updated in place, because the provider's default tags add `project` and `group` and Terraform records `force_destroy = false`. The zone keeps the registrar's comment, "HostedZone created by Route53 Registrar", written in the code so the import does not rewrite it. The owner confirmed the counts with a real plan before applying. |
| 5 | Where the certificate lives | `infra/base`, as ADR 0008, decision 17 planned, with a runbook check of its expiry; `infra/base`, re-issued by `demo-up` when it nears expiry; `infra/demo`, created per session | **`infra/demo`, created and destroyed with each session (PH-12).** ACM renews a certificate only while it is associated with a service, such as a load balancer, or has been exported. Since February 2026 public certificates last 198 days and renewal starts 45 days before expiry. A certificate in base would be in use only during demos, so it would usually expire unrenewed, and the next `demo-up` would fail. Issuing one per session takes a few minutes, while RDS takes about ten. A runbook check relies on memory; a `demo-up` that re-issues base's certificate needs CI to change base. Refines ADR 0008, decision 17. |
| 6 | Hardening records | CAA; null MX; neither | **Both.** A **CAA** record (Certification Authority Authorization) lets only Amazon's certificate authority issue certificates for the domain, so no other authority will issue one even if an attacker passes its validation. A **null MX** (`0 .`, RFC 7505) declares that the domain receives no mail, so senders fail at once instead of retrying. A future page served through another authority, such as GitHub Pages with Let's Encrypt, adds that authority to the CAA record; a mailbox on the domain replaces the null MX. <br>**Settled while building:** the CAA record holds `0 issue "amazon.com"` alone: ACM's documentation lists four names for Amazon's authority and says any one of them suffices. <br>✏️ **Refined by [ADR 0044](0044-landing-page-details.md), decision 3:** the apex's record also lists `letsencrypt.org`, because GitHub Pages obtains the landing page's certificate from it, and `szop.dzwoniec.com` has a CAA record of its own with `amazon.com` alone, so the app's name stays with Amazon. |
| 7 | SES's sender identity | `dzwoniec.com`; `szop.dzwoniec.com` | **`dzwoniec.com`.** One identity lets Szop send from the apex or any subdomain (the address is PH-18's choice), with Easy DKIM's three CNAME records. DMARC and SPF at the apex then protect every subdomain, including future apps' names. An identity on `szop.` would need its own records and leave the apex to be protected separately. |
| 8 | DMARC and SPF | — | **DMARC `v=DMARC1; p=reject` on `_dmarc.dzwoniec.com`, with relaxed alignment** (the default), so mail from an address on `szop.dzwoniec.com` still aligns with DKIM's `d=dzwoniec.com`; strict alignment would reject it. **No report address (`rua`)**, since the domain has no mailbox. **SPF `v=spf1 -all` on the apex**: nothing sends with the domain as its envelope sender, because SES uses its own `amazonses.com`, so Szop's mail passes DMARC through DKIM. **No custom MAIL FROM subdomain**, optional per [OP-044](https://github.com/luiki-dev/szop/issues/76). Settles OP-044's PH-10 part. |
| 9 | The image registry | — | **One repository, `szop`, with immutable tags** ([ADR 0008](0008-hosting.md), decision 13), **basic scanning on push** (free; scanning in CI stays with [OP-090](https://github.com/luiki-dev/szop/issues/194)), ECR's default encryption and `force_delete = false`, so Terraform will not destroy a registry that holds images. **The lifecycle policy** ([OP-013](https://github.com/luiki-dev/szop/issues/66)): rule 1 selects images with a tag starting with `v` and expires only beyond 9999 of them, which never happens; rule 2 selects any image and expires all but the newest 10. ECR has no "keep" action, and an image matched by a higher-priority rule cannot be expired by a lower one, so rule 1 shields released images. A release image carries its commit hash and its version on the same digest, so it matches rule 1. <br>**Settled while building:** rule 2 still counts the images rule 1 shields: it ranks every image by push time, releases included, so an image without a `v*` tag is kept only while it is among the 10 newest overall. AWS's documentation says so: lower-priority rules "can still identify" images a higher rule marked. The count stays 10, since releases are few; rule 2's description and the code's comment say what it really keeps. <br>✏️ **Refined by [ADR 0042](0042-releases-details.md), decision 6:** release images carry their version without a `v` (`0.1.0`), so rule 1's pattern is `*.*.*`. |
| 10 | The Better Auth secret | The owner creates it with one AWS CLI command and Terraform knows only its name (the roadmap's plan); Terraform writes it through a write-only argument | **Terraform writes it, and the value never enters state.** An `ephemeral "random_password"` (64 letters and digits, so it never needs quoting) feeds the write-only `value_wo` of an `aws_ssm_parameter` `SecureString` named `/szop/better-auth-secret`, encrypted with the AWS-managed key. Ephemeral values are never stored in state or plan files; state keeps only `value_wo_version`. **Rotation** bumps `value_wo_version` and applies; the next app start reads the new value, which signs everyone out. The hard rule of [OP-016](https://github.com/luiki-dev/szop/issues/75): **secret values never enter Terraform state; a resource takes its secret through a write-only argument, or the value is created outside Terraform**. The **check**: after the first apply, the value read with `aws ssm get-parameter --with-decryption` is not found in `terraform state pull`. Terraform 1.16 meets the 1.11 minimum write-only arguments need. Completes ADR 0008, decision 22; settles OP-016's PH-10 part. The owner's CLI step is dropped. |
| 11 | The log group | — | **`/szop/app`, 7-day retention** ([ADR 0008](0008-hosting.md), decision 27), default encryption. PH-12's task definition writes to it. |
| 12 | Layout of the code | Flat files by concern in one root module, as in `infra/bootstrap`; local child modules; community registry modules (`terraform-aws-modules`) | **Flat files by concern.** Every resource is written out and readable top to bottom, as ADR 0008, decision 4 asks. Child modules would each have one caller; registry modules hide what the phase is meant to teach, the reason ADR 0008 rejected ECS Express Mode. **The domain is a constant in `locals`**, like bootstrap's bucket name: it is public and never varies. |
| 13 | What the domain serves while no demo runs ([OP-092](https://github.com/luiki-dev/szop/issues/202)) | Nothing, explained in the README; a candidate phase; a page built in PH-10; a scheduled phase | **A scheduled phase, PH-34 "Landing page and demo status", after PH-13 in Stage 3, depending on PH-12.** `dzwoniec.com` always serves a static page; while the demo is down, `szop.dzwoniec.com` redirects to it, and the page shows whether Szop is up, with a link when it is. The owner wants it, and it depends on PH-12, which a scheduled phase records and a candidate does not. It brings its own concepts (static hosting, probably a CDN and a cross-origin status check) and changes how `demo-down` treats the `szop` record, so it does not grow PH-10. Candidates in general stay as they are. <br>✏️ **Refined by [ADR 0044](0044-landing-page-details.md), decisions 1 and 2:** the page is on GitHub Pages, not behind a CDN, and `szop.dzwoniec.com` does not redirect to it while the demo is down: the redirect needed a second repository or CloudFront, and the owner left it out. `infra/demo` and `demo-down` keep treating the `szop` record as before. |

## Consequences

- [PH-12](../roadmap.md#ph-12b-network-and-the-demo-up-button) creates the certificate for `szop.dzwoniec.com` and its validation record in `infra/demo`, and its deploy role ([PH-11](../roadmap.md#ph-11-ci-access-to-aws-and-the-teardown-safety-net)) may request and delete ACM certificates and write that record, besides the `szop` record.
- [PH-18](../roadmap.md#ph-18-registration-and-login) chooses the sender address on `dzwoniec.com` or a subdomain, and verifies the recipients the owner tests with, since SES stays in its sandbox. The apex publishes a null MX (decision 7's records), so the sender address goes on a subdomain (for example `szop.dzwoniec.com`; DKIM's `d=dzwoniec.com` still aligns under relaxed alignment), or PH-18 drops the apex's null MX: [RFC 7505](https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc7505#section-4.2) lets receivers reject mail whose From domain has a null MX.
- [PH-34](../roadmap.md#ph-34-landing-page-and-demo-status) serves a page on `dzwoniec.com`, hosted on GitHub Pages; the CAA record gains Let's Encrypt's authority for it ([ADR 0044](0044-landing-page-details.md), decision 3). `szop.dzwoniec.com` does not redirect to the page.
- A future app on `dzwoniec.com` adds its records to the zone from its own code, finding the zone by name, and sends mail only DKIM-signed for the domain, or DMARC rejects it.
- Costs: the zone $0.50 a month, the domain about $15 a year, a few cents for images and logs.
