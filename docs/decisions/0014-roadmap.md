# ADR 0014 — Roadmap: stages, phases and their order

- **Status:** ✅ Accepted — decision 4 refined by [ADR 0017](0017-readme-roadmap-diagram.md) (the README's roadmap diagram mirrors the stage tables)
- **Date:** 2026-10-02
- **Extends:** [ADR 0004](0004-implementation-process.md), decisions 5, 6 and 8 (the roadmap's structure, the size of a phase and the order of the phases); [ADR 0010](0010-ci-cd.md), decision 23 (the rollout, decided here)
- **Refines:** the consequences of [ADR 0005](0005-development-environment.md), [ADR 0007](0007-testing-strategy.md) and [ADR 0008](0008-hosting.md) (the test tooling, `compose.yaml` and the SES email sender arrive with their first use, decision 7) and of [ADR 0013](0013-visual-design.md) (the UI foundation opens the MVP, decision 9)

## Context

Every foundation topic is decided ([ADR 0005](0005-development-environment.md) to [ADR 0013](0013-visual-design.md)). [ADR 0004](0004-implementation-process.md) asked for a roadmap next: `docs/roadmap.md`, a living document listing the phases in order, each with its status and links to its requirements, spec and plan (decision 6). It already fixed the shape, a walking skeleton first and then vertical slices (decision 5), and how heavy each phase's process is (decision 8). [ADR 0009](0009-git-workflow.md) made a phase one branch and one PR, and asked the roadmap to keep phases small enough to review (decision 3).

What was still open, in the [open points register](../open-points.md) ([OP-002](../open-points.md#op-002), [OP-003](../open-points.md#op-003), [OP-004](../open-points.md#op-004), [OP-031](../open-points.md#op-031)):

- how the roadmap is structured, and what an entry holds;
- how big a phase is: the unit the owner takes through brainstorm, spec, plan and execution;
- how the walking skeleton and the CI/CD work split into phases small enough to review, in an order where no demo runs without the nightly teardown ([ADR 0011](0011-design-sanity-check-follow-ups.md), decision 2), and which real phase each phase name in the ADRs means;
- in which phases the owner's manual steps happen;
- the order of the MVP features, and what happens to the Later and optional work.

The owner first agreed on the structure, the contents of an entry and the size of a phase, before any phase was named, so the roadmap would not have to be reworked; then on the phases, stage by stage. Szop is a learning project: the owner wants to understand each element as it arrives, and expects to split phases further once the spec, plan and execution cycle is familiar.

The roadmap itself is [`docs/roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md). This ADR records why it looks the way it does.

## Decisions

| # | Topic | Options considered | Decision and reasoning |
|---|-------|--------------------|------------------------|
| 1 | Structure | A flat list of phases; stages and phases; three levels (stage, requirement area, phase); phases grouped by requirement area | **Two levels: stages and phases.** A **stage** is an ordered milestone with an exit criterion, such as "every 🎯 requirement delivered, `1.0.0` released". It only groups phases: it has no brainstorm, branch or PR of its own. A **phase** is the work item. Tasks are not a third level: they live in the phase's plan. A flat list of some thirty phases says nothing about where the project stands and hides the MVP boundary. A third level would restate the requirement areas, which phases cut across anyway ([ACC-1](../requirements/functional-requirements.md#acc-1) comes early, [ACC-7](../requirements/functional-requirements.md#acc-7) late), and is a project-management tool rather than a document. Grouping by requirement area loses the build order. **Work outside phases** (dependency updates, documentation fixes, ADR-only PRs) is not on the roadmap. |
| 2 | Stages | — | **Five stages, then candidates.** (1) **Local foundations:** the toolchain and CI. (2) **Walking skeleton, running locally:** SPA → API → database, every test layer and the container image. (3) **Walking skeleton, deployed and released:** AWS, the push-button demo, the nightly teardown and `v0.1.0`. (4) **MVP:** every 🎯 requirement, ending with `1.0.0`. (5) **Later:** every 🔜 requirement. **Candidates** are optional technical phases, not scheduled (decision 13). The walking skeleton of ADR 0004, decision 5 spans stages 2 and 3, so there is a milestone before any AWS work: the slice works locally and in CI before it meets the cloud. The release numbers are those of ADR 0009: `v0.1.0` after the walking skeleton, `1.0.0` when the MVP is done. |
| 3 | How far ahead to plan | Every stage split into phases now; only the next stage; rolling-wave planning | **Rolling-wave planning:** near work in detail, far work roughly. Stages 1 to 4 are split into phases now, since their requirements and decisions are known. Stage 5 holds one rough phase per requirement area ([SHR](../requirements/functional-requirements.md#sharing-shr) and [SYN](../requirements/functional-requirements.md#live-updates-syn)), split once the MVP is done and has taught us things. Splitting only the next stage would leave the MVP's order, and with it the home of every open point, undecided. Splitting Later now would decide details without the feedback ADR 0004, decision 1 wants. |
| 4 | What a phase entry holds | — | **Each stage has a table of its phases** (ID and name, status, spec, plan, PR), the one place where a phase's status is kept, **and each phase a short entry:** its **goal** (what works when it is done, which is also what its demo check looks at); **what it delivers** (requirement and use case links, marked "part" when it delivers only part of one; for tooling and infrastructure, what it builds); **what it depends on**, when that is more than the order; **the owner's steps** (decision 11); the **expected path**, full or bounded (ADR 0004, decision 8), which the phase's brainstorm still decides; and a link to **its group in the open points register**. **Nothing else:** requirement text, open points, designs, tasks and reasons each have their own home ([ADR 0011](0011-design-sanity-check-follow-ups.md), decision 4), and the roadmap links to them. **Status:** ⬜ Not started, 🚧 In progress, ✅ Done, ✖️ Dropped. <br>✏️ **Refined by [ADR 0017](0017-readme-roadmap-diagram.md), decision 4:** the README's roadmap diagram mirrors the stage tables. |
| 5 | Phase IDs | Names only; numbers renumbered when the order changes; stable IDs | **Stable IDs, `PH-01`, `PH-02` and so on, never reused**, like the open points' `OP-` IDs, so open points, PRs and commits can refer to a phase. The order is the order in the document. **A phase that is split becomes `PH-16a`, `PH-16b` and so on, and its old ID is retired**, so the order stays readable; **a new phase gets the next free number**. Names alone break links whenever a phase is renamed; renumbering breaks every reference. |
| 6 | Size of a phase | One phase per requirement; one per requirement area; one per use case or capability | **A phase is one superpowers cycle, one branch and one PR (ADR 0009, decision 3), and it is the right size when:** (a) **it ends with something working** that the owner can see: a feature, or for tooling a command, a check or a deploy, and from the first deploy on it is checked on the demo; (b) **it brings at most one big concept new to the project**, such as Drizzle with migrations, Better Auth or Terraform, which applies `CLAUDE.md`'s "go step by step" to the size of a phase; (c) **it stays reviewable**: its plan has about 3 to 8 tasks, one commit each; (d) **feature phases are cut by use case**, one use case or a coherent part of one, end to end, and the phase that completes a use case adds its E2E journey (the [definition of done](../development/definition-of-done.md), item 1); (e) **infrastructure and tooling phases are cut by capability.** **A phase whose brainstorm or plan grows past about 10 tasks or a second new concept is split on the roadmap before it is planned or executed, and the owner can ask for a split at any time to keep up**; the split is recorded on the roadmap in that phase's PR. One phase per requirement would mean many tiny phases: the MVP has 47 requirements, several of them one sentence long. One per area would be too coarse: [ACC](../requirements/functional-requirements.md#accounts-and-guest-mode-acc) alone covers guest mode, accounts, importing guest data and deleting an account. |
| 7 | When tools arrive | One setup phase installing every tool; each tool with its first real use | **Each tool arrives with its first real use.** Vitest comes with the API skeleton (PH-03), PostgreSQL in `compose.yaml` with the database (PH-04), Testing Library and MSW with the SPA skeleton (PH-05), Playwright and axe with the first E2E journey (PH-07), fast-check, StrykerJS and `mutation.yml` with the first domain rules in `packages/shared` (PH-15), and the Amazon Simple Email Service (SES) email sender with registration (PH-18). The same holds for CI checks, which join `ci.yml` with the area they check ([ADR 0010](0010-ci-cd.md), decision 23), and for IAM roles, which arrive with the first workflow or service that uses them. The owner learns each tool on a real case, and no phase installs what nothing uses yet; a setup phase installing everything would also break decision 6's one-concept rule. **This refines the consequences** of [ADR 0005](0005-development-environment.md) and [ADR 0007](0007-testing-strategy.md) (`compose.yaml` and the test tooling in "the development environment setup phase") and of [ADR 0008](0008-hosting.md) (the SES `EmailSender` in "the walking skeleton phase"). |
| 8 | Order of stages 1 to 3 ([OP-004](../open-points.md#op-004), [OP-031](../open-points.md#op-031)) | — | **CI comes second, before any application code** (PH-02), with only the checks that have something to check, so every later PR is checked from its first commit. **The skeleton is built locally, one layer per phase, before AWS:** the API (PH-03), the database (PH-04), the SPA (PH-05), the production build with the web security baseline (PH-06), the first E2E journey (PH-07) and the container image (PH-08). **The AWS phases follow ADR 0011, decision 2:** the account and `infra/bootstrap` (PH-09) and the permanent part of `infra/base` (PH-10) are applied from the owner's machine; the PR with CI's access to AWS (the OpenID Connect (OIDC) provider, the plan and deploy roles), the real `demo-down`, an empty `infra/demo` and a placeholder `demo-up` is merged next (PH-11), and one run of `demo-down` is seen succeeding; only then does the first deploy (PH-12) press the real `demo-up` on its own branch. **Releases come last** (PH-13), after release-please's trial in a throwaway repository ([OP-025](../open-points.md#op-025)). **The phase names the ADRs use map to these phases** as listed under [Phase names in earlier ADRs](#phase-names-in-earlier-adrs). |
| 9 | Where the UI foundation goes | The SPA skeleton (PH-05), "the first phase that builds UI" as ADR 0013's consequences put it; the first MVP phase | **The first MVP phase (PH-14, the app shell and design system), after the first deploy.** Until then the skeleton's page stays unstyled. The deploy pipeline is tested sooner, and Tailwind CSS with shadcn/ui on Base UI is a concept of its own, which would make the SPA skeleton a two-concept phase. **This refines [ADR 0013](0013-visual-design.md)'s consequences** ([OP-056](../open-points.md#op-056)). |
| 10 | Order of the MVP | Accounts after every single-user feature; accounts right after list items | **The basics of one user's lists first, then accounts, then the remaining features by use case.** The app shell (PH-14), the read-only seed catalog with the first domain rules (PH-15), the guest workspace with its lists (PH-16) and list items (PH-17) come first. **Registration and login (PH-18) and importing guest data on login (PH-19) follow at once**, the owner's choice: accounts carry the biggest unknown, how Better Auth's anonymous plugin links a guest to an account ([OP-038](../open-points.md#op-038)), and a problem found there late could mean a large rework of the workspace model. Extending the import later is cheaper: only templates (PH-28) add to it, since categories are matched by name, never imported ([ACC-4](../requirements/functional-requirements.md#acc-4)). **OP-038's spike runs before PH-16's brainstorm**, so the workspace model is built knowing the answer. Placing accounts after every content feature would have let the import cover everything at once, at the price of meeting the biggest risk last. **Quotas and rate limits (PH-20) follow the account phases, as one phase that sets the pattern:** they cover every write path that exists by then, the registration and login limits and the import included, and later phases add their own rows. Until PH-20, registration has no rate limit, which is harmless on a demo the owner runs and that is destroyed every night. The remaining features follow by use case, from shopping (PH-21) to the automatic cleanup (PH-31). Text lengths ([LIM-2](../requirements/functional-requirements.md#lim-2)) are not a phase: each phase puts them in its shared schemas. |
| 11 | The owner's manual steps ([OP-003](../open-points.md#op-003)) | Left in the ADRs' consequences; listed in the phase entries | **Each phase entry lists the owner's steps it needs**, so none is left only in an ADR ([ADR 0010](0010-ci-cd.md), consequences). For example, PH-09 lists creating the AWS account, securing its root user with multi-factor authentication (MFA) and setting up IAM Identity Center. The owner's items of the [definition of done](../development/definition-of-done.md) (applying `infra/base` from the branch, the demo check) are not repeated in every entry; an entry names them where a phase needs them for the first time or in an unusual way. |
| 12 | Spikes | A phase of their own; a step before the phase that needs them | **A spike is not a phase:** it answers a question and keeps no code, so it needs no branch or PR. It is listed under the phase's "depends on" and runs before that phase's brainstorm: Better Auth's anonymous plugin ([OP-038](../open-points.md#op-038)) before PH-16, release-please with merge commits ([OP-025](../open-points.md#op-025)) before PH-13. A significant decision coming out of a spike is recorded in an ADR by its phase. |
| 13 | Later, candidates and feature ideas ([OP-029](../open-points.md#op-029), [OP-030](../open-points.md#op-030), [OP-040](../open-points.md#op-040), [OP-060](../open-points.md#op-060), [OP-061](../open-points.md#op-061)) | — | **Later holds two rough phases:** sharing (PH-32), then live updates (PH-33), since live updates matter only once a list has more than one viewer. **Candidates are optional technical phases that change no behavior**, unordered and without an ID until the owner decides to do one: load testing with k6 (OP-029), supply-chain provenance (OP-030), Storybook (OP-060, to look at again after the first few feature phases) and what an always-on deployment would need ([OP-033](../open-points.md#op-033)). **Feature ideas stay in the requirements**, where new behavior starts (ADR 0004, decision 10). **Offline check-off stays a 💡 future extension** rather than moving to Later, answering OP-040's question: queuing changes means syncing them and resolving conflicts, a large concept [NET-1](../requirements/functional-requirements.md#net-1) deliberately avoids. **An installable app** (OP-061) joins the future extensions, next to the offline use it depends on. |

## Consequences

- **[`docs/roadmap.md`](../roadmap.md) exists** with the 33 phases and the candidates. `CLAUDE.md` and the README's documentation list point to it.
- **The open points register is regrouped by stage and phase.** An entry that spans several phases sits under the first one and says which part belongs to which phase; the other phases point to it, and each phase moves it on once its part is done. **Every entry becomes a heading of its own** (`open-points.md#op-041`), so links survive when an entry moves; the links in the ADRs, the audit's resolution markers and the guides now use them. **OP-002, OP-003, OP-004, OP-031 and OP-061 are closed**, and OP-040's roadmap question is answered.
- **The functional requirements and the README** list an installable app among the future extensions.
- **The status lines** of ADR 0004, ADR 0005, ADR 0007, ADR 0008, ADR 0010 and ADR 0013 note this ADR.
- **The guides** (`git-workflow.md`, `phase-walkthrough.md`, `github-settings.md`) name the roadmap's phases instead of "the development environment phase" and "the CI/CD phase".
- **The glossary** gains *stage*, *rolling-wave planning* and *spike*, and *phase* now mentions its ID.
- **The next step is PH-01**, the monorepo and toolchain, starting with its brainstorm from its open points.
